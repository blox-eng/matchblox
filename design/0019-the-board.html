Where the board goes

My position: the board is the home. It takes the place of the chain list that #326 opens on. A chain stays a place you go into, its room, and a card says which chain it belongs to. Three tests, as you asked.

What Jobs would cut

One home. Two lists of the same matches make you choose before you can look. The tabs fall to two, the dock to one line of usage, and the matches at rest lie in a drawer. The one accent on the screen is the thing you do next.

Nielsen

Status is visible: every match is on the screen or counted on it (1). The card names its own key, so you recall nothing (6). esc steps back one level, always (3). The match, the words and the keys are the ones the console has today (4). A match at rest takes one line (8).

What the use shows

Measured on this machine today: 25 sessions. 2 ask, 13 work, 10 are idle, and 14 are held. 15 drive a pull request. So two cards matter, ten should be out of the way, and the pull request earns its own column.

What the sketch proposes

  1. One column for each match, and the leftmost wins. Needs you: it asks, a pull request waits for your merge, a stoker step waits for your go, or its ship is stuck. Then Working, then Shipping (a pull request moves by itself), then Quiet.
  2. You choose the columns. A tap on a column's name hides it, and the others take its room. The header still counts who needs you while that column is hidden, and a tap on the count brings it back. The console remembers the choice for each screen.
  3. The chosen card is the peek, and an answer takes two presses. The card opens in place with the last message and the question. The first 1 or tap marks an option. The same press again sends it. So an old habit or a thumb cannot answer by mistake.
  4. One gold card. The accent marks the chosen card, the count in the header and the open tab. Nothing else.
  5. Notices show in the strip's row. The count in the header never hides. On a phone a notice may take two lines, so its fix is never cut. A key that changed its job says so once.
  6. x kills. p merges. No key changes what it does by the state of a card.
  7. Two densities, one key. v switches verbose cards and one line a match. The peek is the same in both.
  8. The chain is a stub on the card, and a way to narrow the board. Choose a card and the stubs of its chain light, in every column. Tap a stub, or press g, and the board shows only that chain, with its stoker's card above the columns. On a phone the stoker is its own page. esc shows every chain again. The header still counts everyone who needs you.
  9. The dock becomes one strip. One line at the bottom shows usage, the machine or this week. u, or a tap on it, turns to the next, and the fourth turn hides it. It does not turn by itself.
  10. On Pocket the keys live on the belt. The matchbox tray gets a fifth match, Board. The app is on it while the console shows the board, as Commands follows the agent you are in. Home is ◂, the chosen card's keys, then ↑ ↓ ⏎. The rest is one drag away. The two rows the key line took go to the cards.
  11. Each card names its harness. The mark sits beside the wait: ✻ Claude Code, ❋ Codex, ⬢ Cursor, ▣ OpenCode, · any other. They are the marks the accounts use today.
  12. A host is a filter, not a place. The board opens on all hosts. s, or a tap on the host's name at the top, narrows it to one. A card from another host says so.
  13. Not in the sketch: the setup steps, the new-session row and the recommendations. They keep their place above and below the board.

Machine, and one layer for hosts

Press tab in the sketch. Machine is the one place you drill into: accounts and their burn, context, the work grid, repos and the machines. Each row opens with ⏎ and comes back with esc. Inside, each has its charts: an account's 5-hour window and its week by day, the fullest match's context, a repo's work by day, a machine's CPU and memory over 24 hours.

Does one layer click?

Yes. A host is where a match lives. It is not a place you go. Going back to Hosts and in again makes you carry the map in your head. So the host becomes the same thing the chain is: a second fact about a card, and a filter. Hosts as a screen stays only for adding one.

The work grid

One square a day, as on a git profile. The hue is the harness that worked most that day, and the strength is how much. It takes five new colour tokens, and they stay on Machine: the board keeps its one accent.

What it needs that we lack

The service keeps five weeks of plan readings, for Claude Code and Codex only. A grid for every harness needs a small daily record. That is a new data source, which #372 rules out today. A host that cannot be reached must say so on the board, never just lose its cards.

The match on the board

The canon's rule stays: only fire, and a match that needs you, move. On a board the eye also has to follow a card that changes column. Answer flint-auth in the sketch (1, then 1) and watch it land.

Copy goes all the way

Text you copy in the console lands on the clipboard of the machine in your hand. Every hop passes it on: the console, tmux on the host, SSH, the terminal or Pocket, and a tmux on your side if you run one. No exceptions.

How

The console writes the clipboard the way it already does for the pair link: one escape sequence (OSC 52) that travels with the screen. It needs no helper on the far machine.

When a hop refuses

The console never says copied on a guess. It reads the tmux it runs in and names the fix, for example tmux set -s set-clipboard on. For a hop it cannot read, the docs give the one setting of each terminal.

Not verified yet

I have not tested the path from this host through the MacBook's tmux, and I have not checked that Pocket takes the sequence into the iOS clipboard. Both are tests in the plan, before any claim.
